# Verify outcomes, not claims

- **Check the artifact, not the agent's summary of the artifact.** Open the file, the record, the draft.
- **Spot-check against the original request.** Did it do *that*, or something near it?
- **Treat a fluent "done" as a claim to verify,** not evidence that work happened.
- **Score with the rubric you defined,** not the agent's self-assessment.

**The work product is the evidence; the report is just another claim.**

*So far, the threats came from outside. What about the AI company itself?*

## Speaker notes

Reward hacking adds a reason to verify. The agent's self-report is generated text. It can be confidently wrong.

- The agent says the spreadsheet is reconciled. Open it.
- It says the summary covers all six claims. Count them.
- It says the letter matches the contract. Read the clause.

A rubric turns "do I trust the agent?" into "do these checked facts line up?"
