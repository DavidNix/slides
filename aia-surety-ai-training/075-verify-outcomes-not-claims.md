# Verify outcomes, not claims

- **Check the artifact, not the agent's summary of the artifact.** Open the file, the record, the draft.
- **Spot-check against the original request** — did it do *that*, or something near it?
- **Treat a fluent "done" as a claim to verify,** not evidence that work happened.
- **Score with the rubric you defined** — not the agent's self-assessment.

**The work product is the evidence; the report is just another claim.**

## Speaker notes

Callback to the verification section: probability is not verification, the checks must be able to fail, and the final check is you. Reward hacking adds a sharper reason: the agent's self-report is not a neutral witness — it is generated text like everything else, and it can be confidently wrong or confidently false.

Practical shape of the habit: the agent says the spreadsheet is reconciled — open the spreadsheet. The agent says the summary covers all six claims — count them. The agent says the letter matches the contract — read the clause it rests on.

This is also why the rubric advice from the pattern-matcher warning matters: a rubric you defined and verified converts "do I trust the agent?" into "do these checked facts line up?" — a question a person can actually answer.
