# The evidence: Anthropic's copyright docket

- **June 2025:** Judge Alsup's order documents **more than seven million pirated books** acquired through Books3, Library Genesis, and the Pirate Library Mirror.
- The ruling was **mixed:** training on the copies was fair use — but the pirated library was not, and those claims went forward.

| Matter | Alleges | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Bartz* (class action) | Pirated books used for training | **Settled — $1.5B**, approved July 2026 |
| *MacKinnon* (class action, B.C.) | Books3/LibGen/PiLiMi copying and training | Active — amended claim Nov 2025 |
| *Grescoe* (class action, Québec) | Copyright, Québec rights holders | Stayed pending the B.C. case |
| Music publishers — Concord (×2), Sony Music Publishing + Warner Chappell, BMG, Round Hill, Reservoir Media | Song lyrics and recorded-music copying | Active, undecided |
| Authors — Carreyrou, Kwon, Cruz, Shakespeare, Cognella, Chicken Soup for the Soul, Gilbert | Books and authors' works | Active, undecided |

**A dozen-plus matters. One settled — it cost $1.5 billion. The rest are waiting.**

## Speaker notes

The courtroom record here is unusually concrete — actual orders, not allegations. The June 2025 fair-use opinion describes how Anthropic built its central training library: more than seven million books, downloaded from pirate sources. The judge separated two questions: training on the copies was fair use on those facts, but keeping the pirated library was not — those claims were headed to trial with potential statutory damages. Certifying the class in July 2025, the same judge called it "straightforward piracy but at massive scale."

Walk the table to make the breadth land: this is not one lawsuit. One settled class action covering roughly 482,000 works — the widely quoted "$3,000 per book" is a gross approximation, not a guaranteed payout per author, and payment administration is running now. Two Canadian class actions, one active and one stayed (a stay pauses proceedings; it is not a dismissal). Six music-rightsholder matters. Seven author suits, including *Bartz* opt-outs who chose to keep fighting.

Be precise in both directions. This is not "Anthropic lost every case" — the training itself was held fair use on those facts, and the settlement is not an admission of liability. But it is also not a gray area about the library: a federal judge called the acquisition piracy at scale, and it cost $1.5 billion.

Hygiene for the table: pending or stayed is not lost; named-plaintiff suits are not class-action losses — count them separately. The non-IP class actions against Anthropic (subscription usage claims, service degradation, antitrust, biometric privacy) exist but belong to a different story; they do not support the copyright one. The docket grows monthly — this is a snapshot as of late October 2026, and statuses should be verified before presenting.

Why it belongs in this section: it shows what human-created data is worth to an AI lab, and what happens when the way it was acquired is challenged.

Transition: "OpenAI's story is different — and in some ways more unsettling."
