# Costs are rising — plan for it

OpenAI's Pro tiers, 2026:

| Date | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| April 9 | $100/month Pro plan added beside the $200 plan |
| September 10 | New sign-ups and upgrades to the $200 plan paused |
| September 29 (DevDay) | $500/month Pro 500 launched; Pro 200 reopened with a smaller allowance |
| October 30 | Existing Pro 200 subscribers move to the reduced allowance |

**The price stayed, the product shrank:** Pro 100 is still $100 (5x Plus, unchanged), but Pro 200's usage was cut in half — 20x to 10x Plus, with GPT-6 Pro messages going from 200 to 100 a week. The new $500 tier offers 25x Plus.

**Same price, less product. Expect costs to keep rising — especially as these companies IPO.**

## Speaker notes

The pattern to teach: a silent price increase. OpenAI kept the sticker price and cut the allowance — Pro 200 subscribers still pay $200 but get half the usage, and the generous allowance now lives on the $500 tier. Pro 500 is also the only Pro tier with Ultrafast.

The timeline shows the sequence: scarcity (pause sign-ups), then a more expensive option appears, then existing subscribers absorb the reduction. That is what pricing pressure looks like from the inside.

Why it will continue: these companies face enormous compute bills and, as they IPO, pressure to show revenue and margin. Cutting allowances or raising prices is the easiest lever. Assume AI costs per seat trend up, not down.

Practical advice for the organization: budget for rising costs, track actual usage against allowances, right-size the model or plan to the task, and measure cost per verified result — the expensive tier is wasted on work a cheaper one verifies just as well.

Verify current prices and allowances before presenting; they change frequently.
