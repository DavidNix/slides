# Check the source, not the quote

- **Never trust a citation, quote, or link produced from memory.** Open the actual document.
- **Prefer retrieval over recall** — tools that fetch the real source beat a model remembering one.
- **Treat every specific citation as a claim to verify** — especially in regulated work.
- **Quoted text enters the record from the held document,** not from the model's rendition of it.

**If it matters, it comes from the document — not from the model.**

## Speaker notes

The habit in one sentence: ask for the source, then ignore the model's version of the source and open it yourself.

Practical shape: ask for the passage with its location — page, section, clause — then find that location in the held document. If it is not there, the citation failed, and that finding is valuable on its own.

Prefer grounded modes when they exist: retrieval and web search attach real documents to the context window, and the model works from those instead of memory. That is the "current sources" idea from the two-hard-limits slide, applied as a habit.

For regulated work — filings, statutory references, contract language — make it a workflow rule rather than a personal habit: quoted text enters the record only from the held document.

Tie to the section: fabrication is not an attack, but it produces the same end state as one — wrong content trusted as authority. The defense is the same posture: inspect, verify, keep authority out of generated text.
