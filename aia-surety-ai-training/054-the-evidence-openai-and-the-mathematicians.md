# The evidence: OpenAI and the mathematicians

- **September 2026:** OpenAI announces an internal system proved the **Navier–Stokes blowup** — a Millennium Prize problem.
- Mathematicians **Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge** had private drafts of closely related results — and had been placing them **in Codex** throughout the project.
- Asked whether the model had trained on those sessions, OpenAI did not answer at the time. Buckmaster: **"I do not know whether our data was used."**
- After an investigation, OpenAI said the sessions could not have influenced the system and acknowledged the pair's priority — **but that is OpenAI's own account, not an independent audit.**

**Work-in-progress typed into someone else's tool is out of your hands.**

## Speaker notes

On September 8, 2026, OpenAI announced that an internal system had produced a proof of finite-time blowup for the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations — one of the Millennium Prize problems. Within days, mathematician Tristan Buckmaster (NYU) published a statement: he and Levent Alpöge had obtained related results in August, had kept their drafts in Codex throughout the project, and had asked OpenAI directly whether its model had trained on, or could access, those sessions. He reported receiving a denial of user-data lookup but no answer on training — and said plainly: "I do not know whether our data was used."

Two days later, OpenAI updated its announcement: an investigation had confirmed the prompts could not have influenced the system, the proofs differed significantly, and the pair deserved priority on the related result. Hold that account carefully: it is the company's own investigation, not an independent audit, and the research behind this deck found no public evidence resolving the question either way. This is a documented dispute about research priority and confidentiality — not established theft.

The lesson does not depend on how it resolves. Two researchers doing the most sensitive thing in their field — unpublished breakthrough work — put it into a hosted AI tool as a matter of course. Once typed, it lives on someone else's servers under someone else's policy. For your own work in progress — drafts, client matters, deal terms — the moment it is typed into a consumer tool, treat it as material the vendor could hold.

Transition: "Why would a lab even want that data? Because data is the gold."
