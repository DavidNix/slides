# The evidence: Anthropic and the authors

- **June 2025:** Judge Alsup's order documents Anthropic acquiring **more than seven million pirated books** through Books3, Library Genesis, and the Pirate Library Mirror.
- **July 2025:** certifying the class, the same judge describes **"straightforward piracy but at massive scale."**
- The ruling was **mixed:** training on the copies was fair use — but the piracy claims survived toward trial. The acquisition was the problem, not the training.
- **Result:** the *Bartz* class action settled for **$1.5 billion** — court-approved July 2026, covering roughly 482,000 works. The settlement is not an admission of liability.
- **Still open:** Canadian copyright class actions (British Columbia active, Québec stayed), and publisher and author suits — Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell, Concord, BMG, Reservoir Media, and several authors — none yet decided.

**Even fair use doesn't cover how the data was acquired.**

## Speaker notes

The courtroom record here is unusually concrete — actual orders, not allegations. The June 2025 fair-use opinion describes how Anthropic built its central training library: more than seven million books, downloaded from pirate sources. The judge separated two questions: training on the copies was fair use on those facts, but keeping the pirated library was not — those claims were headed to trial with potential statutory damages.

Class certification followed in July 2025 — "straightforward piracy but at massive scale" — and the parties settled: $1.5 billion, with final court approval in July 2026. About 482,000 works are covered; the widely quoted "$3,000 per book" is a gross approximation, not a guaranteed payout per author, and payment administration is running now.

Be precise in both directions. This is not "Anthropic lost every case" — the training itself was held fair use on those facts, and the settlement is not an admission of liability. But it is also not a gray area about the library: a federal judge called the acquisition piracy at scale, and it cost $1.5 billion.

The open matters are accusations, not findings: a pending or stayed case is not a loss, and several of the publisher and author suits are named-plaintiff actions rather than class actions — count them separately. The non-IP class actions against Anthropic (subscription usage claims, service-degradation, antitrust, biometric privacy) exist but belong to a different story; they do not support the copyright one. Verify statuses before presenting — these are moving targets.

Why it belongs in this section: it shows what human-created data is worth to an AI lab, and what happens when the way it was acquired is challenged.
