# Data is the gold

- **Data was the gold from the dawn of machine learning.** Spam filters learned from millions of labeled emails; speech recognition from recorded voices; translation from parallel texts. No data, no learning — that principle has never changed.
- **LLMs took it to full scale:** they learned language from a large slice of the public internet — text, code, conversations. There is no ChatGPT without the internet's data to train it on.
- **The easily mined ore is thinning,** so labs chase new veins: licensed archives, user conversations, agent sessions, workplace documents.

**Compute is the shovel, talent is the miner — data is the gold. Your conversations are the newest vein.**

## Speaker notes

Machine learning has been "learning from data" since the beginning: every earlier system in daily life — spam filters, search ranking, speech recognition, machine translation — was built from collected examples. What changed with LLMs is scale: instead of curating examples, labs trained on a huge slice of the public internet — web pages, books, code, forums. The models are that data, rearranged by training. Remove the internet's text and there is no GPT.

That is why the courtroom record on the previous two slides exists: data is the asset everyone is racing for, and the courts have now put a price on it. Public web data is largely mined — scraped, litigated, or running out — so the new veins are licensed archives, user conversations, agent sessions, and workplace documents. View the default-on training settings through this lens: your conversations are the ore being collected right now, whether or not you intended to donate it.

Transition: "Which brings the question home: what happens to your data?"
