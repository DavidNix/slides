# The final check is you

- **Verification ends with a person.** Deterministic checks and a second model narrow the problems; neither accepts the work. Judgment does.
- **It depends on the risk.** Start with one question: what could go wrong if this output is wrong?
- **A large consequence needs a human decision.** Money moves, external messages, claim decisions, anything hard to undo — a person decides.
- **Most things need a human decision.** Approval, correction, or rejection by a person is the one control that fits every task.

**The model can produce; only you can accept. Verification is your judgment, sized to the risk.**

## Speaker notes

Name the section's throughline here, right after the judge slide: nothing taught in this section — checks that can fail, a second model as judge, verification built into the workflow, or the demo we will check shortly — removes the person from the decision. Each tool reduces what the reviewer has to hunt for; none can decide whether the output is good enough to act on.

The sizing question: what could go wrong, and how bad would it be? A draft only you will read needs a skim. An external message, a payment, a claim decision, advice a client relies on — those need a person who reads the evidence and decides. When the consequence is large, the decision itself stays human: the model can prepare the recommendation, but a person accepts, edits, or rejects it.

Be direct with the room: most things need human decision making. That is not a limitation of the model — it is where accountability lives. Before a workflow runs, name the person who accepts each consequential output.

Transition: judgment decides, but it does not scale as an afterthought. The next slide shows how to build these checks and reviews into the workflow itself, so verification happens as part of the work.
