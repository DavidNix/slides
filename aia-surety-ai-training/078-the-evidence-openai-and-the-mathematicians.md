# The evidence: OpenAI and the mathematicians

- **September 2026:** OpenAI announces an internal system proved the **Navier–Stokes blowup**, a Millennium Prize problem.
- Mathematicians **Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge** had private drafts of closely related results, and had been placing them **in Codex** throughout the project.
- Asked whether the model had trained on those sessions, OpenAI did not answer at the time.
- After an investigation, OpenAI said the sessions could not have influenced the system and acknowledged the pair's priority. **But that is OpenAI's own account, not an independent audit.**

**Work-in-progress typed into someone else's tool is out of your hands.**

## Speaker notes

On September 8, 2026, OpenAI announced its system proved Navier–Stokes blowup. Days later, Buckmaster said he and Alpöge had related results in August, kept drafts in Codex, and asked OpenAI whether it trained on them. He got no answer on training: "I do not know whether our data was used."

OpenAI's investigation said the sessions had no influence and gave the pair priority. That is the company's own account. No public evidence settles it. This is a dispute, not proven theft.

The lesson holds either way. Two researchers put unpublished breakthrough work into a hosted tool. Treat drafts, client matters, and deal terms the same: once typed into a consumer tool, the vendor may hold them.

Transition: "Why would a lab want that data? Public data is mined out."
