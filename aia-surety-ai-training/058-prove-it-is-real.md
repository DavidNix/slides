# Prove it is real

- Call back on a number you already have — never one from the message.
- Dual approval for payments and access changes.
- Treat urgency as a signal to slow down, not speed up.

**Authority is proven through a channel the message did not come from.**

## Speaker notes

This is the mitigation for convincing fraud: independent verification. The packaging can be perfect and the request still false, so the process — not the message — carries the assurance.

Out-of-band confirmation means verifying through a channel the suspicious message did not supply: the phone number in your directory, a face-to-face question, the known finance mailbox. A callback to a number printed in the fraudulent email verifies nothing.

Dual approval gives consequential actions a second, independent person: two people approve a payment or an access change, so a single deceived employee is not enough to complete the fraud.

Urgency is the attacker's tool — pressure exists to stop exactly the verification that would expose the fraud. Make it safe and expected to slow down.

Follow AIA's actual reporting process; confirm the contact before presenting rather than inventing one.
